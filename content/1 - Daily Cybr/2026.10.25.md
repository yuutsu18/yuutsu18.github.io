## Mini Study


#### Intro

The way a state sees the concept of privacy can be (very) different from one state to another state.
Today, we will think a bit about the philosophy of two states for which their privacy philosophy is diametrically opposed: China and France (and the whole UE)

#### On one hand

Firstly, we will take a look at China's way of thinking about this topic:
They have a unique philosophy to govern and provide many advantages that are such efficient, fluid, and appreciated by people.
In order for them to offer services like centralized/fluids tx, a reactive police, a low percentage of criminality, fast changes, to innovate this fast in technology (we can think about their recent shocking AI models), science, and more... they have to be paid at a cost. And this cost is the people privacy. In fact, China is the country with the most digital surveillance. Everything is tracked and under the government control: from social medias (QQ, WeChat) to the daily life.
Nevertheless, Professor Ariane Ollier-Malaterre mention Chinese people who express frustration, fear and anger about this system.

#### On the other hand

Now, let's take a look at UE philosophy and more particularly France case:
Countries such as France (that takes into account the GDPR), have a completely different opinion on privacy (here digital privacy). The constitution refuses to acquire personal data / ask for private data as an entry ticket to a more efficient security and/or (to) get the advantages that China have.
This can be easily explained because of the historical context. Indeed, during WW2, the French government (lead by Marechal Petain) was with the nazi Germany. They were over a strong control, every personal data was checked, and because of their private data being under the government control, some were send to forced work camps.
This has made the things change and can explain France's choices on the privacy question. France prefer slow changes, a less efficient/reactive police, etc... while maintaining private data respect (on paper).
Though, we can mention recent multiple cases of cyber criminality and large private data leaks in french government/ministers  websites/services, because of a huge negligence in the security of them (e.g. The basic IDOR vuln in the ANTS site that lead to ~11 millions personal data being leaked ).

#### Synthesis

Comparing both cases makes a recall that no philosophy is better, they both originate from a historical context specific to each country.
My global opinion is not very clear: 
As a cybersecurity student, I tend to prefer France's philosophy about privacy while as a Human, I tend to prefer China's fluidity, QoL,...
